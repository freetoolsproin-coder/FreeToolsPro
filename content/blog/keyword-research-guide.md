Keyword research connects **user language** to pages you can defend. For a tools site, the best opportunities often look like jobs-to-be-done: “calculate EMI,” “generate robots.txt,” “compress image for web.”

## Start from intent, not volume alone

| Intent | Example query | Best asset |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Do | “sip calculator online” | Tool page |
| Learn | “how robots.txt works” | Tutorial / guide |
| Compare | “emi vs loan eligibility” | Guide linking tools |

High volume informational queries without a product angle may not convert. Prefer keywords where FreeToolsPro can deliver the answer **and** the action.

## A lightweight research process

1. List tools you already ship—each is a seed keyword family.
2. Expand with questions (how, what, vs, template, example).
3. Check SERPs: if page-one is dominated by strong tools + guides, plan both.
4. Map one primary keyword per URL; use secondary phrases naturally.
5. Draft titles with the [Blog Title Generator](/social-media-tools/blog-title-generator), then human-edit.

## On-page follow-through

- Put the primary phrase in the H1 and early intro without stuffing.
- Generate baseline tags with the [Meta Tag Generator](/developer-tools/meta-tag-generator).
- Support with [internal links](/blog/internal-linking-guide) from related hubs.
- Validate technical health via a [Website SEO Audit](/developer-tools/website-seo-audit).

## Tool-led content clusters

Example cluster:

- Tool: robots generator  
- Tutorial: [Robots.txt Guide](/blog/robots-txt-guide)  
- Adjacent: [Sitemap Tutorial](/blog/sitemap-tutorial), [Technical SEO Guide](/blog/technical-seo-guide)

Clusters beat random posts that never link to a workspace.

## What to skip

Chasing vanity head terms you cannot satisfy, keyword stuffing footers, and publishing thin pages for every synonym.

Good keyword research ends in a calendar of **pages with jobs**—not a spreadsheet that never ships.

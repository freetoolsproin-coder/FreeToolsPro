Converting PDF to Word unlocks edits when someone sent you a “final” file that still needs a typo fix. Browser conversion is enough for many text PDFs; scanned image PDFs need OCR thinking instead.

## Best candidates for PDF → Word

- Digitally created PDFs (exported from Word, Google Docs, or design tools) with selectable text
- Simple layouts: headings, paragraphs, basic tables
- Single-column articles and letters

## Harder cases

- Multi-column magazines and complex forms
- Heavy vector graphics and text-on-path
- **Scanned** pages (photos of paper)—you get an image in a Word shell unless OCR extracts text

For pure image text extraction, see [OCR Explained](/blog/ocr-explained) and the [Image to Text Extractor](/image-tools/image-to-text-extractor).

## Steps on FreeToolsPro

1. Open the [PDF Converter](/pdf-tools/pdf-converter).
2. Upload the PDF.
3. Choose Word / DOCX output when available.
4. Download and open locally.
5. Fix fonts, spacing, and tables before you treat it as the new master.

## After conversion checklist

- Styles may flatten—reapply Heading 1/2 if you need a TOC.
- Headers/footers can become floating text boxes—clean them.
- Keep the original PDF as the legal source if signatures matter.

## Combine with other PDF jobs

Merge packets first with the [PDF Merger](/pdf-tools/pdf-merger) only if you need one Word file from many PDFs—or convert separately and assemble in Word for cleaner styles.

Conversion is a bridge, not a perfect round-trip. Plan a few minutes of cleanup for anything client-facing.
